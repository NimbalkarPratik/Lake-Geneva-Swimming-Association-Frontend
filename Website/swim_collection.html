Guided Classique

The International Swim across Lake Geneva

13km between Lausanne and Évian-les-Bains. This border-busting adventure traverses Lake Geneva at its widest point — a must for serious open-water swimmers and an ideal setting for a swimming holiday. Swim solo, in relay or as a group.

Swim Route

How to Attempt

  1. Choose your dates — Book as early as possible (no later than 31st March). Bookings cover the entire swim window; specific days cannot be selected.
  2. Secure your dates — Agree on T&Cs and pay a non-refundable deposit.
  3. Complete paperwork — Swimmer Information form, LGSA Medical and Self-Assessment Swim forms (after 1 Jan, at least 2 weeks before window start).
  4. Meet deadlines — Deposit upon booking; balance within 90 days or before 30 April. Registration may be cancelled if incomplete by 31 March.
  5. Late applications — Possible by arrangement only. Speak with the LGSA Secretary first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the route?
Lausanne (Switzerland) to Évian-les-Bains (France). Direction depends on weather. Your escort boat guides you.
When is the best time to swim?
Warmest water is typically July–August (18°C–25°C). Water quality is rated 'excellent'.
Is there a time limit?
Typical completion: 3–6 hours. Maximum limit: 7 hours.
Are wetsuits allowed?
Yes. Wetsuit swims are recorded under a separate classification.
Can I swim in a relay or group?
Yes. Groups of up to 4 swimmers (must swim same pace) or relay teams.
Is it a race or event?
No — it's a private guided swim arranged for you/your group.

Ultimate Challenge

The Signature

The Ultimate Trophy Swim Across Lake Geneva

~70km from Chillon Castle to Bains des Pâquis, Geneva — twice the length of the English Channel. Made entirely of fresh alpine water. Considered one of the toughest open-water swims in the world. Average duration: 28 hours.

Book Now →

Swim Route

Self-Assessment Requirements

Solo attempt: Two swims over two consecutive days — 10 hours (day 1) and 7 hours (day 2) in water ≤18°C, without exiting, no wetsuit or buoyancy aids. Completed within 18 months of your swim window.

Relay attempt: Two 2-hour swims on the same day in water ≤18°C, same conditions. Team leader uploads all forms.

Training Considerations

📏
Distance — 70km+, over 2× the English Channel
🌡️
Cold — Temps can drop to ≤18°C at night
🤢
Nausea — Seasickness prevention essential
🌙
Night swimming — Near-complete darkness likely

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements?
Certified self-assessment swim: 10-hour + 7-hour swims on consecutive days (solo) or two 2-hour swims same day (relay), in water ≤18°C without wetsuit.
How long does it take?
Maximum 36 hours. Average ~28 hours (~2.5 km/h).
Is the water clean?
Water quality 'excellent'. Temps 18°C–25°C, but can change abruptly.
Do people not make it?
Some don't, due to fatigue, hypothermia, or conditions. As Churchill said — success is going from failure to failure without loss of enthusiasm.
Do you provide support crew?
No — support crew is your responsibility. Essential to have a reliable crew who knows your habits.

Swim Route

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a race?
No — it's an adventure swim celebrating your achievement. Not timed competitively.
Will there be feed stations?
Yes, every ~2km for the 6.2km distance only (water, energy bars, gels).
Can I bring my own kayaker?
Yes, but they integrate into the safety team. Personal feeding kayakers allowed additionally.
Can I wear a tow-float?
Mandatory for 6.2km and all skins swimmers. Optional for wetsuited 1.5km/750m/200m.
Is there a cut-off time?
6.2km: 36 min/km cut-off. Last swimmers finish around 1pm. This is a hard stop.

About the Barques du Léman

Meet the largest lateen sailing boats in the world! The original Savoie was built in 1896. The current replica "Savoie 513" honours its ancestor. The Neptune dates from 1904 and was gifted by the State of Geneva for its preservation. Step aboard and let your lacustrine adventure begin!
